# Beginner's Mind

## Shunryu Suzuki's Teaching

"In the beginner's mind there are many possibilities, but in the expert's mind there are few."

This simple statement from Zen master Shunryu Suzuki points to one of the most important qualities for awakening.

## The Expert's Problem

The expert knows. And knowing is the problem.

When we think we know what something is, we stop looking. We see only our concept, not the thing itself.

This applies to spiritual understanding too. Once we think we understand non-duality, we stop looking. We have a concept - "awareness" - and we stop investigating.

## The Beginner's Advantage

The beginner doesn't know. This not-knowing is open, receptive, fresh.

When we approach each moment with beginner's mind, we see it directly - not through the filter of previous understanding.

## The Practice

Can you look at something familiar - your hand, a tree, a loved one - as if for the first time?

Can you approach the question "What am I?" without any answer, as if you've never asked it before?

This is beginner's mind.

## The Paradox

The more we think we understand, the further we may be from truth.

The one who says "I don't know" may be closer than the one who says "I understand."

## The Invitation

Forget everything you've learned. Just look. What is this moment, really? What are you, really?

Don't answer from memory. Look fresh.
