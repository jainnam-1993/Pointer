# Adyashanti

## The Life (1962-)

Steven Gray grew up in an ordinary California suburb. He discovered Zen at age nineteen and practiced intensively for years with little apparent progress.

At age twenty-five, while walking in a forest, he had a profound awakening experience. Several years later, his teacher authorized him to teach under the name "Adyashanti" (Sanskrit for "primordial peace").

## The Teaching

Adyashanti's teaching is honest and grounded:
- Don't trust spiritual experiences
- Awakening is just the beginning
- Integration is the real work
- Be radically honest with yourself

## The Contribution

Unlike many teachers who emphasize only the positive aspects of awakening, Adyashanti speaks openly about:
- The challenges after awakening
- The ways ego can co-opt spiritual experience
- The importance of embodiment
- The ordinariness of enlightenment

## Key Concepts

**Spontaneous awakening:** Not achieved through effort

**Post-awakening challenges:** Integration into daily life

**Embodied realization:** Not just an understanding but a lived reality

**True Meditation:** Having no goal or technique

## His Words

"The primary task of any good spiritual teaching is not to answer your questions, but to question your answers."

"You must want to know the truth more than you want to feel good."

## The Honesty

Adyashanti is known for not playing the role of enlightened master. He speaks of his own continuing discoveries and challenges, making his teaching refreshingly authentic.
