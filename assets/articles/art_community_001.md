# The Role of Community

## The Buddha's Teaching

The Buddha included sangha (community) as one of the Three Jewels: Buddha, Dharma, Sangha.

Awakening may be solitary, but the path is often supported by community.

## The Benefits

**Mirroring:** Others reflect what we cannot see in ourselves.

**Accountability:** Commitment to a group supports practice.

**Validation:** Sharing experiences confirms we're not crazy.

**Transmission:** Some teachings only come through relationship.

## The Challenges

**Groupthink:** Communities can develop blind spots.

**Hierarchy:** Power dynamics can be unhealthy.

**Dependency:** Community can become a substitute for individual inquiry.

**Drama:** People bring their stuff, and projections fly.

## Skillful Participation

Engage without losing yourself. Stay connected to your own direct experience.

Use the community as support, not as authority.

Be willing to leave if it becomes unhealthy.

## Solitary vs Community

Some prefer solitary practice. That's valid. The inner teacher is always available.

But occasional contact with others on the path can be helpful for perspective.

## Online vs In Person

Online communities offer accessibility and anonymity.

In-person communities offer embodied presence and relationship.

Both have value.

## The Ultimate Sangha

In the deepest sense, all beings are your sangha. Awareness meeting awareness in every interaction.

When separation dissolves, community is not a thing you're part of - it's what you are.
