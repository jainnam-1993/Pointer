# Beyond Self-Image

## The Construction

From childhood, we build a self-image. "I am smart/dumb, attractive/ugly, successful/failing, good/bad."

This self-image is a mental construct made of memories, beliefs, and opinions.

## The Suffering

The self-image must be constantly maintained and defended.

When it's threatened, we feel anxious. When it's confirmed, we feel relief. But relief never lasts.

## The Investigation

Ask: What is this self-image made of?

Examine it. It's made of thoughts - about past events, future hopes, other people's opinions.

Is it what you actually ARE? Or is it a mental construction?

## The Discovery

The self-image is not what you are. It is an object in awareness - a bundle of thoughts.

What you are is the awareness in which self-image appears.

## The Freedom

When self-image is seen as a construct, it loses its power.

You can still have preferences and characteristics. But you don't need to defend a fiction.

Criticism hits a character, not you. Praise inflates a character, not you.

## The Practice

When you feel defensive, ask: "What self-image is threatened?"

When you feel pride, ask: "What self-image is being confirmed?"

See that both are movements in awareness, not facts about what you are.

## The Life Without Image

Life without defending a self-image is remarkably free.

You can be wrong. You can change. You can be seen. There's nothing essential at stake.
