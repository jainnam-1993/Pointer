# The Role of the Guru

## The Traditional View

In traditional Advaita, the guru is essential. The teaching is transmitted from realized being to student.

"Without the guru, there is no awakening."

## The Modern View

Modern non-dual teaching often downplays the guru.

"You are already awakened. No teacher needed."

## The Integration

Both views are partial.

The guru IS needed - as a mirror, a pointer, a presence that evokes recognition.

The guru is NOT needed - because what you seek is already here.

## What the Guru Does

The guru:
- Points to what you are
- Reflects your true nature
- Challenges your assumptions
- Doesn't let you settle for partial recognition

## What the Guru Doesn't Do

The guru doesn't:
- Give you enlightenment (you already have it)
- Make you dependent (true gurus free you)
- Claim to be superior (same awareness everywhere)

## The Inner Guru

Ultimately, the true guru is awareness itself.

External gurus point to this inner guru.

When the inner guru is recognized, external gurus are honored but not needed.

## The Danger

The guru-student relationship can be abused:
- Projection creates false authority
- Power corrupts
- Dependence replaces freedom

Stay alert. Trust your own direct experience above any teacher.

## The Gratitude

And yet, when a true guru appears, gratitude is natural.

Not worship of a person, but appreciation for the pointing.
