# How to Work with Koans

## What Are Koans?

Koans are stories, questions, or statements used in Zen training. They are designed to frustrate the rational mind and provoke direct seeing.

Famous examples:
- "What is the sound of one hand clapping?"
- "What was your face before your parents were born?"
- "Does a dog have Buddha nature? Mu!"

## How NOT to Use Them

Don't try to figure out the answer intellectually. The mind will create clever responses, but these miss the point.

The koan is not a riddle with a conceptual solution.

## How to Use Them

1. **Take one koan.** Work with it exclusively.

2. **Hold it in awareness.** Let it percolate. Take it with you throughout the day.

3. **Become the question.** Don't think about it - be it.

4. **Let go of finding an answer.** The frustration of not-finding is part of the process.

5. **Wait for breakthrough.** It may come suddenly, unexpectedly.

## The Breakthrough

When koan breakthrough happens, there is no answer in words. There is a direct seeing, often accompanied by laughter.

The answer cannot be spoken because it is not conceptual. You can only show it.

## Traditional vs Solo Practice

Traditionally, koans are worked with under a teacher who can confirm genuine insight.

Solo practice is possible but requires radical honesty about whether you're fooling yourself with clever answers.

## Start Here

Take the koan "What is aware right now?" Work with it today.

Don't answer. Just look.
