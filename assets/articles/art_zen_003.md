# Kill the Buddha

## The Statement

"If you meet the Buddha on the road, kill him." - Linji

This shocking statement is one of the most famous in Zen. What does it mean?

## The Surface Meaning

Obviously, it doesn't mean literal killing. The Buddha here represents our concept of enlightenment, our image of what we're seeking.

## The Deeper Meaning

Whatever concept of awakening you hold, let it go.

If you think awakening is bliss - kill that Buddha.
If you think it's peace - kill that Buddha.
If you think it's knowing you are awareness - kill that Buddha.

Any image of enlightenment becomes an obstacle to it.

## Why?

Because enlightenment is not an experience or state that matches some image. It is the direct seeing of what is - before any concept.

The moment you have a concept of what you're looking for, you're looking for a concept rather than truth.

## The Application

In practice:

When a spiritual experience arises and you think "this is it" - kill that Buddha.

When you understand something profound - kill that Buddha.

When you think you've arrived - especially then, kill that Buddha.

## The Freedom

Real freedom is freedom even from the concept of freedom.

Don't settle for any Buddha. Keep looking until there is nothing left to look for and no one left to look.

## The Warning

This doesn't mean nihilism or rejecting everything. It means not getting stuck - in anything.
