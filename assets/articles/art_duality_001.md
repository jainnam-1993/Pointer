# Subject and Object

## The Division

From earliest childhood, we learn to divide experience:
- Subject (me) and object (everything else)
- Seer and seen
- Experiencer and experienced

This is the fundamental duality.

## The Problem

This division creates the sense of separation. "I" am here; "it" is there.

From this sense of separation comes all psychological suffering.

## The Investigation

Look at your current experience.

There seems to be a seer (you) seeing an object (screen, words).

Now: Can you actually find the boundary between seer and seen?

## The Discovery

When we look carefully, we can't find where "seer" ends and "seen" begins.

Seeing happens, but the division into subject and object is added by thought.

## The Collapse

In direct experience, prior to thought:
- There is no experiencer having experiences
- There is just experiencing
- No inside, no outside
- Just THIS

## The Implication

If subject and object are not separate:
- There is no "me" separate from life
- There is no "world" separate from awareness
- Everything is one seamless happening

## The Living

This doesn't mean we stop using "I" and "it." Language needs these distinctions.

But we know they are useful fictions, not fundamental truths.

## The Practice

Throughout your day, notice the sense of "me here, world there."

Ask: Can I actually find this boundary?

What happens when I can't?
