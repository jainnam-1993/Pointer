# From Mindfulness to Awakening

## The Starting Point

Mindfulness has become mainstream. Being present to current experience with acceptance.

This is valuable. But it's not the end.

## Mindfulness

Mindfulness typically involves:
- Attention to present experience
- Acceptance of what arises
- Non-judgmental awareness
- Returning when distracted

This helps calm the mind and reduce suffering.

## The Limitation

Mindfulness usually leaves the subject unexamined.

There is someone being mindful of something. The sense of "me" watching experience is not questioned.

## The Bridge

From mindfulness, we can ask: What is aware of this experience?

This shifts attention from the content of experience to the fact OF experience.

## The Awakening

Awakening goes further. Not just noticing experience, but recognizing what is aware.

The question shifts from "What am I aware of?" to "What is awareness itself?"

## The Discovery

When we turn attention to awareness itself, we find:
- It has no form
- It has no boundary
- It is not personal
- It is what we are

## The Integration

Mindfulness doesn't disappear with awakening. Attention to present experience continues.

But it's informed by the recognition of what is aware. The sense of separate "me" being mindful dissolves.

There is just awareness, being aware.

## The Invitation

If you practice mindfulness, let it mature into inquiry.

Who is being mindful? What is awareness itself?

Let the practice deepen into recognition.
