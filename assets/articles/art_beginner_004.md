# Why We Suffer

## The Root Cause

All psychological suffering has one root: the belief in being a separate self.

This separate self feels:
- Vulnerable (it can be hurt)
- Incomplete (it needs something to be whole)
- Anxious (the future is uncertain)
- Defensive (it must protect itself)

## The Mechanism

The separate self is created and maintained by thought. Every moment, thought says "I" and refers to a body-mind as "me."

This is useful for daily functioning. But it becomes problematic when we believe it absolutely.

## The Evidence

Consider: when you are deeply absorbed in something you love, does the sense of self feel heavy or light? Usually light - or even absent.

When you worry about yourself, does the sense of self feel heavy or light? Usually heavy.

The separate self IS the weight.

## The Alternative

What if the sense of separation is not actually true? What if it is a useful fiction, like a character in a movie?

The movie character feels very real to itself. But it is made of light on a screen.

## The Invitation

This is not about destroying the self. It is about seeing its nature clearly.

When we see that the separate self is a construction of thought, we don't lose anything real. We lose only the suffering that comes from taking it to be absolutely true.

## The Beginning

Start by noticing: when is the sense of separate self strongest? When is it lightest?

This noticing itself begins to loosen the grip.
