# Taking Refuge

## The Search for Safety

We seek refuge constantly:
- In money (security)
- In relationships (belonging)
- In achievement (worth)
- In knowledge (control)

These offer temporary refuge. They can be lost.

## The True Refuge

Is there a refuge that cannot be lost?

What you are - awareness itself - is always here. It cannot be taken away.

## The Practice

In difficult times, ask: What is aware of this difficulty?

Turn attention to that awareness. Rest there.

This is taking refuge in what you are.

## The Three Jewels

Buddhism speaks of taking refuge in:
- Buddha (awakened nature)
- Dharma (the teaching)
- Sangha (community)

These point to one refuge: awareness recognizing itself.

## External and Internal

External refuge: teachers, teachings, community.
Internal refuge: your own awakened nature.

External supports pointing to internal.

## The Shift

When we take refuge in what we are:
- We can face any circumstance
- We are fundamentally safe
- Nothing essential can be lost

This is not denial of difficulty. It is recognizing what remains stable in any storm.

## The Invitation

What is your refuge? Where do you look for safety?

Is it something that can be lost?

Can you find the refuge that is never not here?
